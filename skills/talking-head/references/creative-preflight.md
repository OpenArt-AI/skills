# Creative preflight: preserve truth without freezing the video

## Start from the material the user has

A product image and a short brief are a valid starting point. Author the missing concept, script, performance
and shot direction; do not require a reference video or a user-written screenplay as the price of good work.
Video references are optional aids for an adaptation, identity, delivery or timing. When absent, design those
relationships explicitly from the audience, message and product evidence. Do not pretend to have observed
an unavailable reference. This starting point does not override this skill's delivery boundary or model limits.

Inspect supplied image pixels before directing interaction. Separate product facts and identity from framing,
pose, background and lighting. An identity reference is not automatically a mandatory first frame or a fixed
camera. Preserve known shape, label, count and supported functions without copying incidental stillness.
For an unknown mechanism or claim, seek necessary evidence or select a supported action and viewpoint.
Do not solve uncertainty by removing all interaction or by inventing a feature.

## Give the sequence a reason to develop

Before writing the model prompt, identify the intended opening, development and resolution, proportional to
the requested format. For each meaningful beat, state what the viewer discovers and how it becomes visible
or audible. Useful change may come from a person's intention and response, product interaction, camera path,
focus, light or environment within the continuous presenter shot. These are choices, not a checklist
of effects or a motion quota. A deliberate still moment can support attention and comprehension.

Direct positive, observable behavior instead of relying on adjectives such as natural, dynamic or premium.
For camera motion, describe the useful start/end relationship and reveal; for performance, connect the line's
meaning to attention, reaction and an appropriate physical task. Avoid a gesture on every word, arbitrary
micro-movements or an exact tiny angle that does not serve the idea. A fixed camera does not require a fixed
person, product or emotional register. Breathing and blinking alone are not a performance plan.

Budget an ending for the actual message, action, reading or emotional resolution. Do not append a default
four-second hold or a fixed closing percentage. When a hold is needed, identify its purpose and the supported
behavior during it. Preserve required copy and sufficient reading time; shorten redundant lead-ins first.

## Audit constraints before submission

Default to positive camera, performer, product-action and shot-scale direction. The continuous presenter
shot, no internal cuts and no B-roll are this skill's defined delivery boundary; keep those constraints.
Do not extend them into blanket bans on the person's movement, gaze, emotion or supported product handling,
or allocate unsupported long holds. Audit inherited prefixes, negative prompts and templates: additional
constraints need the user's actual words, an approved script or a genuine feasibility reason. Testing
convenience, generic premium/restraint language, a single image or fear of generation errors does not justify them.

Resolve factual or feasibility uncertainty with evidence, a focused question or an appropriate
positive action plan. Do not expand an unknown mechanism into a motion ban for the whole film.
Describe what should happen, then remove unnecessary negative direction. Format boundaries still
apply: describe a continuous Talking Head take positively, without freezing the person,
product, gaze or emotion. Model-native internal cuts also fall outside this presenter-take boundary.

Check what the viewer learns and why each beat is worth watching. If prohibitions or holds erase
progression, revise before submission. Follow explicitly requested static frames, no-cut takes or
long holds, retaining their actual user source.

## Review the resulting experience

Play the whole result with sound. Judge motivated progression, believable performance, useful product evidence
and the ending separately from identity, geometry and technical validity. Record timestamped failures such as
an inert opening, repetitive posing or an unexplained hold, and revise the responsible direction within scope.
A passing prompt check, transcript, contact sheet or successful render is not proof of engaging motion or
natural delivery. If playback is unavailable, leave those checks unverified.

## Apply this to continuous presenter footage

Keep every generated clip a continuous presenter shot, with no internal cuts or B-roll. A stable professional
camera is valid. Improve the person and explanation within that frame; do not cure stiffness with a montage,
gratuitous camera travel or constant gesturing. Permit meaningful gaze changes, emphasis, pauses and supported
product handling. Direct reactions to ideas rather than a uniform tiny hand gesture repeated across products.

Image-only example: in a stable desk shot, the presenter states one buying question, looks at the product to
identify a visible detail, lifts the closed item closer when explaining that detail, and returns attention to
the viewer for the answer. Use a fictional presenter if appropriate; a reference performance is optional.
The deliverable remains independent presenter footage, not an assembled finished advertising edit.
