# Music beneath a clear, human explanation

## Choose a supportive treatment

Normally include light, product-appropriate background music in presenter-led promotional footage, even when
the brief does not mention music. Camera-facing delivery can feel warm, lively or reassuring. Choose energy,
pulse and texture from the product, the audience, the presenter and the pace of the explanation. Do not reduce
professional sound to a generic corporate loop or assume a stable continuous shot must have no music.

A practical demonstration with momentum may suit a restrained drum pulse; a thoughtful explanation may suit
a softer, spacious bed; a welcoming product introduction may feel gently upbeat. These illustrate reasoning,
not mandatory genres, instrument lists or category presets. Music should support attention and the presenter's
point of view without supplying all the emotion or disguising thin copy.

## Make unscored exceptions deliberately

Explicit dialogue-only or no-music instructions take priority. Podcast conversations, interview-style exchanges,
documentary treatments and technical demonstrations in which the product's sound is evidence often call for
voice and natural ambience alone. Decide from the commissioned treatment, not just a format keyword: a podcast
promo can have a scored introduction while its conversation remains unscored. Preserve an explicitly requested
dry take for later editing. Write a brief reason for an unscored choice; do not omit music merely because the
brief is sparse, the camera is fixed or the output is an individual presenter clip.

## Keep the explanation in front

Use instrumental or nonverbal accompaniment under speech unless vocals serve an explicit creative purpose.
The voice should be effortlessly intelligible at normal playback volume, with clear consonants, natural pauses
and consistent perceived level. Keep the bed perceptible but behind the speaker; simplify or ease it back for
dense information, quiet reactions or meaningful product-operation sounds. Rhythm supports the thought rather
than forcing faster speech or mechanical emphasis. Preserve useful room tone instead of confusing it with BGM.

Shape an unobtrusive entry and complete ending within the take. Avoid a loud finish, abrupt loop restart or
truncated cue at the last word. Use qualitative energy and mix relationships rather than default BPM, fixed
decibels, mandatory instruments or arbitrary fade lengths. A continuous musical bed does not authorize cuts,
B-roll, graphical additions or any other change to the continuous-presenter boundary.

## Generate and verify the intended sound

Put a concise music decision into the treatment and each full generation prompt: presence or deliberate absence,
emotional character, pulse/texture, balance with voice and meaningful sounds, and ending behavior. Keep it separate
from exact dialogue. Check the live model's native audio support and supported controls; do not invent a BGM
toggle or disable all audio to suppress unwanted music. Music must be generated inside the original clip.
This skill does not add an external track, remix or assemble footage in post-production.

For separate takes, carry the same musical character when appropriate, but do not promise identical tracks,
seamless music across clips or isolated stems. If a later edit requires dry dialogue, follow that output request
and preserve the reason for omitting music. Do not claim the generated mixed audio can be cleanly separated.

Listen to each complete result for actual music presence, fit, speech clarity, competing vocals, unwanted level
changes and a complete ending. Confirm silence from music when an unscored take was chosen. Missing planned BGM
is a review failure; a native audio stream or successful speech alone is insufficient. Correct within authorized
scope or identify the unresolved limitation. Leave musical balance and delivery unverified without listening.
