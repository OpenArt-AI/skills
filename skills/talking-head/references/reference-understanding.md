# Understand a reference without importing its edit

Measure duration, dimensions, frame rate, rotation and audio streams with available inspection tools.
Inspect the entire video, including its opening, progression, payoff and ending. Listen to the performance
where playback is available. A transcript supplies words, not facial reactions, vocal emphasis or lip sync.

Use time-labeled frames to locate scenes, then inspect denser ranges around meaningful gestures, reactions
and changes. Pair speech with timestamps. Classify what is actually present:

- Presenter: identity, gaze, expression, posture, gestures, framing, light, clothing and environment.
- Delivery: words, pace, pauses, emphasis, vocal qualities and relationship to the listener.
- Editorial treatment: cuts, punch-ins, B-roll, demonstrations, captions, graphics and music.
- Argument: opening promise, explanation, evidence and ending.

Keep observations separate from interpretations. For example: "The speaker pauses after the question and
leans forward on the answer" is observable; "this invites the viewer into a private discovery" is an
interpretation that may guide the new take.

Translate only relevant relationships into this output. A long source can inform delivery without requiring
the same total duration; a requested full-length speech must not be shortened to fit a model default.
An edited source's multiple shots are not a reason to generate cuts inside a clean presenter take.
Read the full source to understand context, but do not recreate its B-roll, overlays or transitions.

Create a compact reference note with source identity, inspected ranges, observations, intended transfer,
excluded editorial elements and unresolved checks. A supplied video is not automatically sent to the model:
pass it only when its role is necessary and the live schema supports it. Keep the creative reference distinct
from the target presenter's identity recording.

If playback, audio or source access is unavailable, identify exactly what was inspected. Request the missing
material when essential, or proceed with explicit limitations when the user's task still permits it.
Do not describe full audiovisual understanding from a contact sheet alone. No Hypit ingestion, WhisperX
alignment or composition project is required; available transcription can assist analysis but is not mandatory.

## Read how the recommendation is written

Keep a timestamped transcript, marking uncertain ASR words rather than silently guessing them. Divide by
changes of thought, not only shots. For each useful passage record the actual wording, its conversational
job, the implied everyday situation, the reason to recommend, how it follows the preceding sentence,
the concurrent product action, and the understanding it adds for the viewer. A label such as hook/benefit/CTA
does not explain why a line persuades.

Where audio is accessible, inspect pace changes, emphasis, pauses and reactions in context; where it is
not, distinguish measured timings and textual interpretation from heard vocal qualities. Separate an
observed movement from an inference about spontaneity. Check whether an offer or disclosure interrupts
the reasoning or sits naturally inside it, rather than assuming all offers must appear at the end.

Extract the reasoning relationships, not another person's catchphrases, biography, medical claims or
demographic identity. A gift story, first impression, tutorial and sponsored recommendation may teach
different things; do not label every supplied example a verified ad. See
[recommendation writing](recommendation-writing.md) for applying the observations to new copy.
