# Build attention that belongs to the product

Choose one main communication point and a product-connected reason to watch. An unrelated spectacle followed
by a product insert may attract attention without delivering the intended message.

A hook can begin inside an action, pose a concrete question, reveal a surprising but supported relationship,
show a useful demonstration, establish a recognizable situation, or use sound, humor, texture or contrast.
Do not promise a payoff that the rest of the video never supplies.

## Choose the execution

Use the form that best communicates the selected point:
- Action/demonstration when seeing an operation makes the benefit understandable.
- Situation or concise narrative when context explains why the product matters.
- Comparison when the dimensions and evidence are honest and clear.
- Humor or visual contrast when the joke remains connected to the product.
- Product/sensory when visible material, motion or sound is the attraction.
- Graphic explanation when words or annotations materially improve understanding.
- Presenter expression when a person helps convey the message.

These can combine. Do not default every product to talking head, white background or animated selling points.
Cast, props and setting should serve the selected situation. Direct intentional reactions rather than generic
constant excitement, laughter or sales-announcer delivery.

## Give the sequence a reason to continue

Establish a useful expectation and resolve it through the ad. Keep enough time for recognition, key actions
and evidence. Pace can come from contrast, pauses, action continuity or sound, not only fast cuts.
A long demonstration with a well-timed reveal may work better than many incomplete actions.

Choose viewpoint changes, tracking, punch-ins, reaction cuts, jump cuts or speed changes for a reason.
Specify what a cut advances: comprehension, anticipation, humor, action or product recognition.
Do not accelerate every action or randomize zooms to manufacture energy.

## Decide what graphics do

Choose no graphics, restrained emphasis or a graphic-led treatment according to the brief and information.
A sports or movement product does not automatically need stickers, arrows or circles.
Start with the content and identify what is difficult to understand or emphasize.

Treat these as distinct layers:
- Dialogue captions reproduce or summarize speech as commissioned.
- Feature/offer text communicates product facts or the requested action.
- Decorative graphics reinforce energy, material, emotion or a joke.

Use exact words and purposeful placement. An annotation points to a real detail; a motion stroke follows
an intended action. A drawn spark is a graphic effect, not a claim that the product produces sparks.
Keep typography, line weight, accents and movement coherent within a version. Hold information stable for reading.
Do not hide product, face or hand errors behind decoration.

## Use sound and an appropriate ending

Choose product sound, speech, ambience, a pause or a small number of effects to support the event.
This hook set has no BGM at any stage; follow [music and sound](music-and-sound.md).
Keep essential speech and product sounds intelligible. Avoid a sound effect on every edit.
If the placement/brief needs silent comprehension, ensure the core message also works visually.

End with the appropriate resolution: product recognition, an action callback, a joke, a loop or a CTA.
A CTA must use supplied or verified information. Not every awareness ad needs an explicit instruction,
and not every feed ad needs a formal brand end card.
A loop must be intentional, with physical and sonic continuity, rather than an abrupt incomplete ending.

For variants, identify what each direction tests. A new reason to care is a concept change; rephrasing an opening
while preserving the body is a hook change. Do not call three unrelated ads a controlled hook comparison.

## Make the opening promise earn its payoff

Translate a relatable frustration or surprising contrast into an immediate visual and spoken premise.
The next beat must demonstrate why the product matters; do not let the hook promise a result absent
from the body. For creator formats, direct curiosity, recognition and recommendation as reactions
to specific evidence. Compare full opening concepts when that is the experiment unit, not merely
synonyms over the same motionless picture. Keep the accepted body asset fixed as commissioned.
For direct-response briefs, favor a clear product proposition early and a verified next-action CTA.
Consider supplied logo closure after CTA when it suits the format, without a default long hold.
