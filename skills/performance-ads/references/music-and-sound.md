# Music that serves the ad's message

Normally include fitting background music even when a sparse brief does not mention it. Choose its energy,
pulse and texture from the product, audience, performance and intended feeling. A creator recommendation may
need a light bed; a playful demonstration may suit a restrained drum groove; a reassuring explanation may
need softer, warmer movement. A bold visual concept can give music more presence. These are relationships,
not fixed genres by product category. A performance ad does not automatically need aggressive drums, drops,
fast speech or a beat on every cut. Music supports the argument; it cannot replace useful evidence.

## Decide whether scoring helps this format

Honor explicit no-music, dry-dialogue and source-audio-preservation requests. Street interviews, podcast
conversations, documentary exchanges and demonstrations where listening to the product is evidence often
benefit from voice and natural sound alone. Decide from the actual treatment: a promotional introduction may
be scored while its interview remains unscored. State a brief reason for omitting BGM. A static camera, sparse
brief or a requirement to understand the ad while muted does not itself justify an unscored export.

## Plan a musical relationship, not a generic track label

Describe enough mood, pulse and texture to support the specific idea without mandatory instruments, BPM,
loudness numbers or a fixed cue structure. Usually prefer instrumental or nonverbal music under dialogue.
Keep speech effortlessly clear and preserve meaningful product sounds. Ease the bed back for dense information
or a quiet reaction, and let music carry a pause when helpful. Rhythm should support the person rather than
force mechanical delivery. Ambience, operation sounds and BGM are different layers.

Give music an intentional entry, progression and ending within the duration. Avoid an unrelated CTA sting,
restarts at every cut or truncation under the final word. If the creative loops, consider the musical return
as well as the image. Preserve important sound choices during revisions. For a controlled variant, state
whether music is a variable or a constant; matching directions do not guarantee identical generated tracks.

## Put it in the generation and review contract

Include music presence or deliberate absence, character, balance with voice/product sounds and ending in the
treatment and every complete model prompt. Keep these directions outside exact dialogue. Discover actual native
audio support and configure only supported fields; do not invent a dedicated BGM parameter. Generate music
inside the returned video, without promising external soundtrack assembly or local mixing in this workflow.
Resolve an unsupported audio treatment instead of silently dropping it.

Listen to the entire output for actual music presence, product/tone fit, clear words, competing vocals, level
jumps, continuity and a complete ending. Confirm intentional unscored sections too. When muted comprehension
is required, perform that as a separate review; it does not replace the listening pass. Missing planned BGM is
a review failure, even if speech is present. Correct within authorized scope or report the gap. A prompt,
audio stream or transcript alone does not establish musical quality; mark listening checks unverified if unavailable.
