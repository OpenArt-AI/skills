# Intake for a paid social ad

Inspect supplied material and previous decisions first. Determine the audience, their existing knowledge,
the placement/context if specified, and the action or understanding the ad should create.
A brief can be sparse; infer sensible creative choices without requiring a marketing questionnaire.

Distinguish the assignment:
- A new ad from product images or facts: choose a grounded angle and execution.
- A detailed brief, script or prompt draft: retain its intended message and required information; improve the hook, evidence, expression and payoff.
- An explicitly locked or approved unchanged plan: execute that version without another hidden rewrite.
- A reference adaptation: preserve requested attention, explanation or payoff relationships.
- An existing output repair: inspect the failed passage and trace it to the submitted inputs.
- Concept variants: explore different reasons to care.
- Controlled variants: change a specified hook, message, format or CTA while holding named constants.
- Prompt-only or analysis: do that work without video generation.

Record the core audience need, product/SKU, one main communication point, available evidence, exact offer/CTA
when supplied, intended duration/aspect ratio, languages, audio needs and locked elements.
Separate the business goal from the creative hypothesis. "Drive purchases" does not automatically justify
invented urgency, a discount or a testimonial.

Classify product images, demonstrations, identity references, brand assets, style/camera references and approved
copy. For software/service ads, identify accessible real evidence rather than treating a plausible generated
interface as proof. Ask for a missing fact only if it changes the promise, execution or essential accuracy.

Recommend a concise duration suited to the hook, evidence and action. Propose a ratio for the actual
placement and obtain approval when unspecified; 9:16 is not an automatic submission setting. Verify
relevant current platform requirements when needed, without assuming universal safe-zone coordinates.

Identify which layers are wanted: speech, dialogue captions, product/offer copy, decorative graphics,
music and effects. "No stickers" does not mean "no captions"; "no dialogue captions" does not remove an approved
feature label. Infer the clear intent and clarify only real conflicts.

Summarize why the audience would stop, what they should understand, and how the ending serves the goal.
These questions guide the treatment; they do not force a rigid hook-problem-solution-CTA timeline.
