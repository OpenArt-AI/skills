# Develop the user's intent into a stronger script

Apply this to sparse briefs AND detailed scripts, shot tables, timecoded prompts or drafts from another AI.
Input completeness is not creative quality or approval. Default to interpreting the intended result and
developing the strongest genre-appropriate proposal within the user's framework, rather than transcribing
their first draft. This guide governs creative development before approval; the approved version governs
execution afterward. See [shared video experience](video-experience.md) for the paired Prompt/Script Plan.

## Separate the message from its current expression

Before authoring, identify the audience, intended response, product/subject, main promotion points,
supporting evidence, desired emotional effect and narrative functions. Ask what each passage is trying
to accomplish: establish relevance, show a useful detail, explain why it matters, answer an objection,
present an offer or invite a next step. Retain that useful logic while improving its expression.

Separate three things in a compact working note, without making the user complete another questionnaire:
- Invariants: product/SKU identity, supported facts, amounts/units, offer terms, required information,
  user-designated keywords and exact phrases, explicit exclusions, deliverable limits and genuine locks.
- Intended framework: the relationship among the opening, demonstration, explanation and ending; a
  reference's relevant tone, action or progression. Preserve its purpose rather than every weak sentence.
- Creative material to develop: draft wording, bare slogans, provisional beat timestamps, repetitive
  praise, awkward transitions, thin reasoning and generic camera/performance direction.

Preserve required keywords in their original wording, but do not classify every word in a pasted script
as required. A factual amount must remain accurate even when its surrounding sentence is rewritten.
Track each required message and phrase to its place in the proposed dialogue, image or screen text.
Respect a requested delivery channel: a required spoken offer cannot silently become screen-only copy.
Do not omit a required point merely to make the writing smoother; surface a real duration conflict.

## Determine what is genuinely locked

Exact execution requires an explicit user instruction such as "use these words verbatim," "do not rewrite,"
"these timestamps are mandatory," a previously approved version requested unchanged, or a real dependency
such as supplied voiceover, synchronized choreography, legal copy or an exact source edit.
Keep only that scope locked. Exact offer wording does not lock surrounding dialogue; a fixed total length
does not automatically lock every internal beat. Approval of a concept is not approval of all later wording.

A detailed table, quotation marks, imperatives inside an imported AI draft, or a finished-looking prompt
do not by themselves establish a human request for literal execution. Interpret the user's surrounding
request and actual constraints. When they ask to improve even an approved script, reopen the requested
creative scope and present the revision. When they explicitly request faithful execution, honor it and
surface concerns without secretly replacing their copy. Do not manufacture a conflict simply because a
rough draft is precise. Ask a focused question only when a genuine lock or business intent is ambiguous
and the choice would materially change the result.

## Treat timecodes as a planning framework unless precision is required

For an input such as "0–2s: show product; 2–4s: explain feature; 4–6s: offer," understand the functions first.
Keep the intended progression and important information. Rewrite the dialogue and align meaningful action
with it; an imperative shot description is not necessarily spoken copy. If these are proposed beat windows,
redistribute them for natural speech, useful demonstration and readable text within the requested runtime.
Show the revised times in the Script Plan and briefly disclose any material timing change.

If the user explicitly fixes the windows, improve within those windows. Do not promise frame-accurate
generation from prompt timestamps. If a genuinely fixed duration cannot fit required words, reading time
and actions, show a concrete tradeoff for approval instead of silently extending, deleting or speed-reading.
More developed writing means more meaningful reasoning per moment, not automatically more words or seconds.
PGC/Talking Head timed rows can be changes of thought/action within one continuous shot, not mandatory cuts.

## Apply the skill even when there is already a script

Look for the creative gap: a weak viewing promise, unexplained feature, irrelevant claim, disconnected
offer, unnatural language, static handling or an ending that arrives before the recommendation is earned.
Improve the underlying connection, not merely synonyms or adjectives. Keep the user's product, purpose
and useful framework; do not replace them with an unrelated house concept just to demonstrate creativity.

| Skill | What development should contribute |
| --- | --- |
| UGC | A particular person's relatable situation, a strong opening, specific reasons to recommend, meaningful handling and connected friend-to-friend speech. For intimate sharing, consider close handheld phone framing when feasible, rather than automatically seating a formal presenter in a domestic room. Enthusiasm follows the discovery; it is not constant hype. |
| PGC | A clear question or proposition, an explanation that makes the supported detail understandable, purposeful same-shot demonstration and a useful conclusion. Keep warmth and conviction; professional presentation does not mean a specification recital. Retain the continuous-footage boundary. |
| Talking Head | A reason to listen, a developing thought and an expressive camera-facing relationship. Use pauses, emphasis, gaze and feasible gestures to support meaning. Non-promotional explanations do not acquire a forced offer or CTA. |
| TVC | A specific brand idea expressed through imagery, product behavior, material, sound and progression. Turn a shot list into a coherent visual experience; do not automatically add narration or convert it to UGC. |
| Performance Ads | A product-connected hook, one clear reason to care, evidence that pays off the hook and an appropriate action. Preserve the controlled variable and agreed constants when developing test variants. |
| Product Listing Video | A coherent answer to buying questions through exact product details, operations, scale and concise annotations. Develop a useful demonstration rather than converting every specification into an animated slogan. |
| Hypit production | A stronger message and relationship between presenter, supporting footage, captions and sound. Preserve retained source performances and exact-edit constraints; adapt only the commissioned creative scope. |

Develop life relevance from confirmed facts, observed operations or explicitly authored situations.
Do not invent purchases, weeks of use, results, testimonials, credentials or unsupported sensory claims
to make speech feel human. Friendly wording and a generated demonstration are not evidence of efficacy.
Examples teach reasoning, not a fixed persona, catchphrase, hook type or script structure.

## Example: improve a detailed six-second draft without expanding it

Illustrative brief: a fictional desk lamp has a verified touch dimmer and costs $29. The user supplies
"0–2s: Bright light! 2–4s: Touch dimming! 4–6s: Only $29!" and wants a six-second UGC recommendation.
The price must appear on screen; exact slogan wording and beat boundaries are not explicitly locked.

The intent is a quick useful demonstration leading to an affordable offer. Develop that intent:

| Approximate time | Proposed speech | Picture and all added screen text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | "Too bright?" | Close phone view of the person and lamp already on the desk; the person reacts to the light. No added text. |
| 2–4s | "Tap it down." | A feasible free-hand tap lowers the brightness using the verified control. No added text. |
| 4–6s | "That's better." | The person settles into the more comfortable setting. Exact added text: "$29". |

This converts three labels into a problem, action and reaction while retaining the feature, product,
price, six-second frame and first-image product visibility. It is not a promise that a model will hit
these timings. If the user required "$29" to be spoken, the proposed speech must include it as well.
Do not reuse this lamp scene for other products or manufacture a longer testimonial to show more effort.
For a longer brief, use the available time for additional supported reasons and useful detail.

## Show the improved proposal, then lock execution

Complete all executable prompts and their matching readable Script Plan in the same authoring pass.
Show the improved script, not merely advice to improve the user's original. Briefly explain the meaningful
changes, for example: "I kept your three-part progression, touch-dimming feature and $29 price, and turned
the feature labels into a short demonstration and reaction. The six-second duration is unchanged.
Would you like to use this revised script, or follow your original input exactly?"
Place this short explanation AND explicit version-choice question at the END of the response containing
the Script Plan. Do not hide the changes inside the plan or merely announce an improved script without
asking. Follow the mandatory version-choice step in [generation approval](generation-approval.md).

Do not add a separate "may I rewrite?" gate for ordinary draft development. The existing plan review is
where the user chooses the revised script, their exact original or further changes. Wait for that choice;
approval locks the selected version and generation must not perform another hidden rewrite. A generic
approval waiver is not a silent choice of the rewrite. Honor an explicit delegation of that version choice
within scope, retaining disclosure, paired plan/prompt and required facts. No waiver permits expansion
of paid generation or invention of claims.

Before presentation, review writing independently of performance adjectives: can the viewer understand
why the detail matters, does each beat advance the thought, does the opening get a real payoff, and are
all required messages still present? Faithful facts plus deliberate genre-specific writing is the goal.
Changing every line is not a success criterion, and documentation changes do not prove better A/B results.
