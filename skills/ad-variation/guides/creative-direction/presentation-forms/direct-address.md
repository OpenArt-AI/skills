# Direct address

The viewer reads the presenter's intention through face, voice, gaze, posture, and pauses. Choose the relationship: sharing a discovery, challenging a claim, explaining a difficult step, telling a personal story, or another response grounded in the message. Energy alone does not establish that relationship.

Decide when uninterrupted presence matters and when another picture should lead. A reaction may need an unobstructed face; a complicated step may need a full-screen demonstration. A continuous presenter image is optional. If the presenter remains as an inset, choose shape, scale, position, and movement from the reference and the visible information, not a default circle.

For retained footage, choose coherent takes and protect meaning, breaths, and useful reactions. For generated footage, secure identity and deliverable speech before designing precise word-linked overlays. Frame the performance with later crop and composition in mind, without asking the model to draw those overlays.

Judge the final edit as a conversation with the viewer. Repeated punch-ins or B-roll swaps need an expressive reason. Captions can support accessibility and emphasis without covering the mouth or competing with a demonstration. An understated piece may rely almost entirely on the quality of performance and sound.
