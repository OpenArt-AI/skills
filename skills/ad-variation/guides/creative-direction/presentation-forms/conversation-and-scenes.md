# Conversation and enacted scenes

Map who speaks, who listens, and which response changes the scene. Two people in frame do not automatically require a podcast layout; an interview, remote conversation, street encounter, or short acted situation can have different eyelines, camera relationships, and sound.

Keep identity and voice evidence separate for each participant. Give each reference a distinct role in generation requests. Preserve screen direction, gaze targets, positions, lighting, and acoustic space when continuity matters. Do not use a person reference as proof that a multi-person model can keep roles separate; verify actual model support and outputs.

Read reactions as part of the argument. A listener's pause or expression may be the payoff, while cutting immediately to the next line could erase it. Edit turns and overlaps deliberately. Diarization, if available, provides timing evidence rather than a final editorial choice.

An enacted scene may depend on physical contact, coordinated movement, or cause and effect that separate image references cannot convey. Inspect the useful source-video range and select a model capable of the relationship. When uninterrupted action exceeds capability, distinguish a new edited interpretation from strict preservation.

This Skill can direct presenter-related conversational or enacted passages. A request dominated by specialized tracking, simulation, or long-form dramatic production may require additional capabilities; keep the overall commission visible while identifying the exact missing operation.
