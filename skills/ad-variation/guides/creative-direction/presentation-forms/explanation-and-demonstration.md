# Explanation and demonstration

Ask which evidence the viewer must understand, in what order, and what they need to remember. The presenter may introduce, guide, compare, react, or disappear while the evidence occupies the frame. Picture and speech should divide the work instead of duplicating everything.

A persistent board can keep comparisons available while the speaker moves through them. A sequence of full-screen shots can reveal a process. A ranking can retain already introduced items while moving one into a new position. A product walkthrough can follow input, meaningful interaction, and result. These are possible relationships, not compulsory scene types.

Make the source of timing explicit. Speech-linked reveals follow the relevant phrase or payoff. A silent interactive demonstration needs authored dwell and reading time. A mixed piece may use both, and the composition can transition between them.

Real software evidence follows [product interface](../techniques/product-interface.md). Physical-product evidence follows [supporting pictures](../techniques/supporting-pictures.md) and preserves actual appearance, scale, and use. Avoid presenting a generated or illustrative result as a captured product action.

Coordinate the presenter, evidence and labels when they rearrange together. Keep independently timed elements separate where useful; a dashboard, diagram or persistent comparison should not be forced into a montage template.
