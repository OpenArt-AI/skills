# Caption meaning, timing, and layout

Use the accepted spoken material as timing evidence. Compare automatic transcription with the actual audio and intended copy; correct product names and recognition errors without concealing a mis-spoken line. If speech changes, old word timings are no longer authoritative.

Choose caption behavior from the reference and viewing conditions: readable phrases, active-word emphasis, editorial keywords, speaker labels, or no captions when appropriate to the commission. Full accessibility captions and decorative emphasis serve different purposes; decide what is required.

Group words into readable units that preserve meaning and breath. Avoid dangling articles, orphaned brand names, flashes too short to read, and oversized words that reflow the block during playback. Place the caption in relation to the actual face, UI, and platform safe areas. A bottom-centered block is one choice, not a requirement.

Keep caption content and timing in a simple subtitle file or timing record suitable for the chosen edit. Resolve each word/phrase against the production clock. Use a consistent rounding policy and half-open frame ranges. Trimming, joins, and retiming require updated ranges. See [speech and events](../../composition/speech-and-events.md).

For active-word coloring, test overlap and gaps at frame boundaries. A phrase can remain visible across a short pause while no word is active. If the selected design tracks a face or object, obtain real tracking evidence or author reviewed position curves; do not label a static guessed location as tracking.

Render sample phrases including the longest line and accented or non-Latin characters before the final export. Check font loading, glyph coverage, wrapping, shadow/outline, contrast over both presenter and B-roll, and whether line changes distract from the message. Then review continuous playback for synchronization and readability.
