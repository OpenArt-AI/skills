# Graphics with a reason to move

Start with the idea to be made visible. Name the objects, their relationships, initial state, important changes, and resolved state. A comparison, process, correction, reveal, or persistent board has more useful structure than a collection of animated rectangles.

Separate **when an event occurs** from **how its movement unfolds**. A word or gesture can locate a reveal; a designed easing curve and duration control its entrance. Some graphic sequences have their own reading rhythm and need no speech anchor. Expose separately meaningful internal events instead of placing all motion at fixed fractions of one outer duration.

Coordinate elements that share layout or motion, and let independent elements follow their own timing. Use only the visual treatment needed for the commissioned result; do not build a generic template or component system.

Establish hierarchy through scale, grouping, alignment, contrast, and time. Let the viewer recognize a control before it changes and understand a result before the next reveal. Use overshoot, spring, cuts, holds, or continuous motion according to the reference's attitude; avoid constant movement simply to make a video appear polished.

Review motion as a sequence, not only attractive keyframes: onset, acceleration, overlap, settling, hold, and exit. Check intersections with captions, media cropping, and audio accents. Return to the treatment if polishing a transition cannot fix an unclear idea.
