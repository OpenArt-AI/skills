# Continuity across generated material

Track dependencies as accepted assets and explicit relationships. An identity portrait may feed several shots; a selected speaking recording may feed all performance passages; one accepted final frame may seed a physically continuous next action. Share only what the intended continuity needs.

For each shot or passage, record speaker, voice route, wardrobe, setting, lighting, camera/crop, source references, dialogue, duration, and what may vary. Reuse a concise continuity description while directing each passage's own intention. Uniform enthusiasm across every line is not continuity of character.

Inspect selected outputs before making them references for downstream work. Propagating a subtly wrong face or product detail makes later correction expensive. A boundary frame can help a continuous movement, while a new camera angle or B-roll cut may need the identity reference instead. Do not force identical starts and poses across independent shots.

Plan audio continuity as well as appearance. Match vocal character, acoustic space, level, and sentence boundaries. A model may reset the voice even with similar images; verify the chosen reference-audio path and listen to assembled seams. A good-looking montage does not establish a coherent presenter performance.

Reuse accepted media on graphic-only revisions. If a changed script invalidates a performance and its alignment, regenerate/re-record that passage and recompute dependent events. Keep the unaffected body and supporting assets when they still serve the treatment. Note which accepted files and source ranges were reused.
