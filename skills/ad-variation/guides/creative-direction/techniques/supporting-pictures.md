# B-roll and supporting pictures

Assign each supporting picture a purpose: demonstrate a claim, show a detail, establish context, supply a reaction, or bridge an editorial seam. Select the range of speech or action it supports. A shot that is merely associated with a noun may distract from the argument.

Choose material by evidence and expression. Actual product captures preserve specific facts; supplied footage can preserve a real process; generated footage can realize a new scene; authored graphics can clarify an abstract relationship. Use OpenArt MCP for needed image/video generation and supported composition operations; choose supplementary tools only for operations it does not provide. B-roll need not be generated, full-screen, or present at all.

Plan entrance, duration, crop, and return. Enter when the picture becomes useful, remain long enough to read the evidence, and return for a reaction or transition when the presenter adds value. A long explanation may need several meaningful states rather than a continuous decorative camera move. A persistent comparison may be more effective than multiple cuts.

Keep the speech's timing and sound independent from which picture leads. A presenter can remain audible over supporting material. Decide whether the support's own sound helps; mute irrelevant source audio and avoid accidental doubling. If the overlay covers a cut, check both the audio seam and the visible transition back.

For factual product representation, preserve logos, packaging, geometry, and UI labels using actual assets where available. Mark illustrative outcomes in the production evidence ledger and, when needed to avoid a misleading advertisement, in the visible treatment. Do not invent proof of a feature by generating a plausible-looking interface.

Review the full frame for competition: caption, title, UI, presenter, and sound may all demand attention at once. Reduce or sequence them according to the message instead of adding more layers by default.
