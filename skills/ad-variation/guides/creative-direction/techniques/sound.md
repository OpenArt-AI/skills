# Sound supports the performance

Identify the primary voice, source ambience, music, and effects. Preserve useful recorded voice whenever the commission calls for it. Avoid audio being played once from a video element and again from the audio track; visual media should normally be muted when the separate mix owns sound.

Listen for clipped words, breath discontinuities, room changes, noise, distortion, inconsistent level, and audio/video drift. A short equal-power crossfade or room-tone bridge can help a cut, but overlapping syllables are not repaired by a longer dissolve. Choose editorial boundaries before processing.

## Choose music for the commissioned experience

Normally include fitting background music in a completed ad, even when the brief does not mention it. Derive
energy, pulse, texture and emotional movement from the product, audience, speaker and visual treatment.
Intimate recommendations and explanations usually need a light bed; a playful demonstration may suit a modest
drum groove; a softer moment may need warm, spacious movement. A brand film may let a more developed score lead
its visual passages. These are choices of relationship, not fixed genre assignments, instruments or BPM by
product category. Music supports the story and recommendation rather than compensating for weak copy.

Honor explicit no-music, dry-dialogue and preserved-source-audio requirements. Street interviews, podcast
conversations, documentary exchanges and product-sound demonstrations often work best with voice and natural
sound alone. Judge the actual segment: a promotional intro can be scored while the conversation stays unscored.
Record a brief reason for omitting music. A requirement for muted comprehension does not by itself request a
silent export. Neither a sparse brief nor a continuous shot justifies accidentally leaving out the music.

## Assign responsibility for the music layer

Record the music decision in the treatment: presence or deliberate absence, character, progression, balance with voice and product sounds, and ending. Decide whether retained/generated audio already supplies the score or the final edit will add a track. If music will be added separately, request clean speech and useful natural sound from generated takes so independent baked-in scores do not fight the final mix. A dry intermediate take is not an unscored final deliverable. If generated or retained audio already contains music, inspect it before adding another bed; do not assume its music can be isolated from speech or removed cleanly.

Use supplied or licensed music with recorded provenance, or a supported authorized generation route. Resolve the actual usable audio asset before claiming a complete soundtrack. Keep the selected music file and its used range when needed for further edits. If a usable track is unavailable, report the unresolved dependency instead of marking a silent export complete.

## Mix for comprehension and a complete ending

Usually prefer instrumental or nonverbal music under speech. Keep the voice in front at normal playback volume;
duck or simplify the bed for dense words, quiet reactions and meaningful product sounds. Let it support pauses
without filling every gap. Effects should clarify an action or reveal rather than mark every interface movement.
Do not force faster speech, constant cuts or gestures to follow a beat.

Shape entry, development and resolution across the assembled timeline, avoiding restarts at clip boundaries,
unrelated CTA stings and accidental cutoff under the last word. Music can briefly recede or stop for a useful
sound or silence. Do not apply a universal gain or fade duration without listening. Preserve accepted music
when it is outside a revision's scope; do not change it accidentally during a hook or product replacement.

Measure peaks and loudness where appropriate to the delivery platform. Normalize or limit based on the actual source and target, preserving natural dynamics and avoiding clipping. Do not claim a particular loudness standard was met without measurement. Retimed picture and sound must remain synchronized and retain an acceptable voice.

Check the assembled export with sound, including the first/last syllables and every join. Visual inspection and speech recognition cannot establish acceptable mixing or a convincing vocal identity on their own. Record the distinction when audio audition is unavailable.
Verify that planned BGM is actually audible, suits the product and treatment, stays clear of important words
and sounds, and resolves with the ending; confirm intentional unscored passages too. Missing planned music is
a review failure. A timeline track, generated prompt or audio stream alone does not prove it reached the export.
Correct within scope or report the gap; leave balance and musical-fit checks unverified without listening.
