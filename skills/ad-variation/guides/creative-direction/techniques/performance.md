# Direct a recognizable person and a believable performance

Identity, vocal identity, and performance style are related but distinct. Inspect what the user's recording actually provides. Preserve relevant facial features, proportions, hair, age appearance, and characteristic expression. A wardrobe or location change should be deliberate, not an accidental identity change.

Describe the speaker's response to the thought. "She is pleased to have found a simpler way, and lets the discovery land before demonstrating it" gives voice, posture, and expression a common purpose. Specify a gesture or vocal accent when it clarifies the point. Avoid prescribing a gesture on every phrase or reducing performance to "energetic".

A real speaking video is the preferred reference when continuity of the person's manner matters. Inspect the selected excerpt with sound; choose one showing usable face detail and natural behavior. Supply it in the actual supported video-reference input and state its role. Do not use an unrelated creative-reference speaker's voice or facial behavior as the person's identity.

If exact voice matters, verify whether the selected model uses reference audio, native speech, or a separate audio/performance pathway. The available capabilities determine what can be generated; do not assume a text-to-speech or voice-cloning endpoint exists. Keep usable recorded audio when the request permits. If audio must change, verify the new path and inspect pronunciation, sentence endings, and lip synchronization.

Evaluate an accepted take across its duration: identity on head turns, hand/face integrity, expression, gaze, speech completeness, unintended cuts, framing, and mouth movement. An attractive first frame is insufficient. If a defect is localized, decide whether a valid edit/B-roll cover preserves the message or a new take is necessary. Do not hide incorrect spoken content behind supporting pictures.

Reserve downstream graphics through practical framing: room beside a shoulder, a stable eyeline, or an appropriate crop. Do not describe a future graphic as if the performer should hold or interact with a physical object unless that object belongs in the generated scene.

## Make the recommendation specific

Connect words, handling and reaction: identifying a detail directs the gaze and hand; inviting a
closer look motivates bringing the item forward; a suggested use or gift creates a personal-style
opinion and an appropriate response. Use the actual product's evidence, not copied reference claims.
Choose the relationship first: intimate enthusiastic peer recommendation for UGC, structured
professional explanation for PGC. Tailor cast, setting and tone when missing. Design the opening
promise, evidence and next action before adding supporting visuals. Editing reinforces these
relationships; captions and music cannot substitute for an engaging primary performance.
