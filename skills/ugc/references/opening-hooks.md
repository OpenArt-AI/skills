# Design the opening as speech, image and action

Use for UGC, PGC, Talking Head and other presenter-led promotions. For non-promotional teaching or opinion
videos, keep the attention principles without inventing a product, offer or sales CTA. Brand films and
listing videos retain their own viewing context; do not force a direct-response speaker into every format.

## Give the first 3–5 seconds a useful promise

Decide why this particular viewer would keep watching before refining the rest. Start with a recognizable
situation, pointed question, worthwhile curiosity, supported contrast, visible demonstration or consequential
detail. A rhetorical question is one option, not a mandatory template. Avoid greetings, generic hype,
unrelated shock, empty suspense and a feature/discount recital that delays the actual reason to care.

For a product promotion, make the actual product recognizable from the first image: already in the hand,
being used, or clearly present in the relevant working area. For a digital product, an intelligible supported
interface view can play that role. Do not deliver several seconds of empty-handed speech before retrieving
the item. Naming the brand can come later; early product visibility does not require a spoken sales pitch,
a static packshot or a compulsory logo card. Preserve an explicit user-locked opening and surface conflicts
instead of silently rewriting it. Keep the face, useful action and product readable in the selected ratio.

Design words and action together. Let the image supply tangible context while the words raise the relevant
question or explain the consequence. Use captions when the commissioned format supports them; choose exact
wording in the Script Plan and prompt. Clean PGC footage may intentionally have no added text: retain the
product and opening action, record "no added on-screen text," and do not promise post-production captions.

## Make the next thought pay off the first

A hook creates an expectation the body must satisfy through a supported reason, understandable demonstration
or useful explanation. Do not leave an open loop unanswered. Curiosity does not authorize invented personal
history, clinical results, superiority, scarcity or testimonials. Match vocabulary, emotional stakes and
delivery to the audience. Warmth and conviction can come from a precise observation and reaction rather
than constant excitement or scripted filler words.

Choose the strongest mechanism for the brief. Alternatives may be useful during authoring, but do not
automatically overwhelm the user with many hooks or generate extra videos. For requested hook variants,
pair each opening prompt with its Script Plan and keep the intended body/offer/CTA constants explicit.

## Transferable examples, not reusable claims

| Product/context | Opening example | First image and body obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serum | "Does your skincare pill under sunscreen too?" | The person already holds the actual serum. Continue with a relevant supported explanation or supplied demonstration; this question does not prove that this serum prevents pilling. |
| Milk frother | "How do you get that foam on an iced latte at home?" | Frother and glass are visible as the operation starts. Show the verified operation and explain the detail that matters. Do not turn a still product photo into proof of foam performance. |
| Notes software | "Are your meeting notes scattered across three different places?" | Show the actual supported interface in the format's allowed presentation. Demonstrate the verified organization workflow rather than making unsupported integration claims. |
| Non-product explainer | "Why does this number change when nothing else does?" | Presenter and the relevant example are immediately understandable. Resolve the question; no invented product or promotional ending is needed. |

Adapt the reasoning, language and action to the commissioned product. Avoid copying a reference creator's
catchphrases. For clean presenter footage, keep useful handling in the same shot; a timed action is not a cut.

At planning, check the opening separately: relevance, recognizable subject/product, meaningful action,
credible wording and connection to the body. At media review, watch and listen to those first seconds;
a well-written prompt does not prove that the product appeared or that the hook landed.
