## Why the wording feels personal and watchable

- **A person has a reason to participate.** "I'm not even a coffee drinker, but..." makes curiosity a
  meaningful exception to her stated preference. The viewer understands why she cares. Transfer the logic
  of a particular point of view; do not copy her ten-year coffee history into a new person's mouth.
- **The viewer is invited into a shared activity.** "We're going to try" and the preparation steps give
  the audience something to follow. Product information enters because the activity needs it. Personal
  presentation does not require withholding the collaboration or disguising a commercial relationship.
- **Instructions and reactions do different jobs.** Ingredients, the smoothness cue and assembly supply
  useful detail. "Looks spicy" keeps the open taste question alive. The brief "one more thing" adds a
  meaningful final detail; artificial forgetfulness is not a reusable requirement.
- **The conclusion resolves an expectation.** "Actually not that spicy" answers the anticipated concern;
  "a vanilla latte with a kick" provides a recognizable taste anchor plus the difference. The listener
  gets an imaginable experience instead of a string of positive adjectives.
- **The ending belongs to this conversation.** The invitation to try follows making and tasting. It does
  not suddenly switch to a detached sales command. A required purchase CTA can follow the same principle
  of continuity while preserving its actual wording.

These are observations about language and visible context. Exact vocal emphasis, timbre and pause delivery
have not been directly heard and verified. For a new performance, direct curiosity, concentration during
preparation, and a considered reaction to the result as intentions, not as a claimed exact voice imitation.

Keep the persuasive purpose and find a human content vehicle for it: a task, discovery, comparison,
selection or shared experiment the audience would care about. Let a specific person's reasons and useful
observations connect the required product information. When an offer is required, integrate it into that
