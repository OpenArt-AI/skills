# Read the complete creator reference

Inspect duration, dimensions and audio, then watch and listen to the entire source, including its ending.
Use timestamped frames and transcripts as aids, not replacements for motion, tone and lip-sync review.

Record observations about camera holder and distance, eyeline, exposure, room, light, wardrobe, posture,
breaths, pauses, sentence emphasis, product visibility and handling. Track the opening situation, demonstrated
fact, spoken claim and ending. Separate natural behavior from cuts, captions, music and commercial packaging.

Distinguish observable facts from interpretations: a glance toward the pocket is visible; calling it a
spontaneous discovery is an inference. A scripted or generated reference does not establish real experience.
Inspect hand occupancy, product states and camera changes around demonstrations, not only the speaking face.

Map what transfers: conversational relationship, action timing, camera arrangement and information order.
Explicitly exclude another creator's identity, unverified experience and unsupported product features.
Do not copy a cinematic orbit merely because the reference happens to show a person in a kitchen.

Keep a compact source/range/observation/transfer/uncertainty note. Classify identity, product, style and copy
references separately before upload; only submit necessary media in roles supported by the live schema.
If sound or full video is inaccessible, say what was actually inspected and leave affected conclusions open.

## Read how the recommendation is written

Keep a timestamped transcript, marking uncertain ASR words rather than silently guessing them. Divide by
changes of thought, not only shots. For each useful passage record the actual wording, its conversational
job, the implied everyday situation, the reason to recommend, how it follows the preceding sentence,
the concurrent product action, and the understanding it adds for the viewer. A label such as hook/benefit/CTA
does not explain why a line persuades.

Where audio is accessible, inspect pace changes, emphasis, pauses and reactions in context; where it is
not, distinguish measured timings and textual interpretation from heard vocal qualities. Separate an
observed movement from an inference about spontaneity. Check whether an offer or disclosure interrupts
the reasoning or sits naturally inside it, rather than assuming all offers must appear at the end.

Extract the reasoning relationships, not another person's catchphrases, biography, medical claims or
demographic identity. A gift story, first impression, tutorial and sponsored recommendation may teach
different things; do not label every supplied example a verified ad. See
[recommendation writing](recommendation-writing.md) for applying the observations to new copy.
