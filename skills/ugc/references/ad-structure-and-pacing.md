# Organize the recommendation and fit its pace

## Develop the intent in sparse and detailed inputs

A supplied script, including a detailed beat table, is creative input rather than automatically a locked
contract. Apply [intent and script development](intent-and-script-development.md) to extract the intended
framework, required information and explicit locks. Use [recommendation writing](recommendation-writing.md)
to strengthen reasons, connected speech and meaningful actions within that framework. Honor an explicit
verbatim request or an approved version requested unchanged. Show the complete script, settings and planned
counts under the existing generation approval workflow; reuse authorization already established for this scope.

## Let the viewer follow a useful thought

Establish relevance early through a recognizable situation, intriguing operation, specific question or
worthwhile discovery. Early relevance does not require announcing a brand, benefit claim or discount in the
first sentence. Avoid both generic preamble and a disconnected attention grab. The opening should lead to
the actual conversation that follows, with a reason for this audience to stay.

Choose the organization from the product and listener's decision. A first look can develop through an
expectation and supported observation; a useful task can reveal why a feature matters; a selection can
explain why one option suits a particular person; a routine can give each item a distinct job. These are
possible relationships, not mandatory beats. Claim, problem, proof, offer and CTA can still organize the
persuasion. Express them through a personal perspective, useful activity and connected thoughts that people
want to watch, rather than announcing each as an isolated slogan. This is a change in how the information
is carried, not a rejection of the chain. Use the functions the actual brief needs; do not invent a missing
pain point or discount just to complete a checklist.

Earn the conclusion with specific reasoning. A next step should follow naturally and preserve every
required CTA, code, price or guarantee. An offer can appear early when it explains a relevant decision now;
it must not replace the product's reason to matter. Optional closing invitations should suit the brief,
rather than forcing every reference-inspired story into a sales sign-off.

## Fit useful information, speech and handling together

Estimate duration by reading the exact dialogue naturally in the requested language and delivery style,
including operations, pauses and reactions. Words-per-second estimates are rough diagnostics, not upper
limits, minimum density rules or evidence of a convincing performance. A brisk, dense explanation and a
tactile demonstration need different time allocations. Do not imitate a reference's speed at the expense
of comprehension, or halve its reasoning simply because a generic speaking-rate estimate is slower.

Each additional passage should contribute a reason, observation, instruction, relevant distinction or
decision. Remove repeated praise and filler. For a shorter version, select fewer reasons but preserve the
causal connection that makes each convincing. For a longer version, add useful substance. Do not stretch
a few slogans with slow delivery, tiny motions or a long final hold. If all required wording and meaningful
action cannot fit, surface the duration conflict instead of rushing, dropping text or promising unreadable cards.

Distinguish exact spoken copy, exact visible copy and information requirements before allocating time.
Keep requested text readable without obscuring the demonstration. Preserve any specified card-hold minimums,
and verify actual text and timing in the generated result. Do not add captions against the brief or assume
an overlay can silently replace required speech.

## Review the recommendation before and after generation

First check the speech on its own: does it help the listener understand why this product is relevant,
or merely name features and offers? Then check the complete video for meaningful handling, natural
variation in delivery, intelligibility, complete commercial wording and an intentional ending.
Technical fidelity does not establish persuasive writing or a believable performance. Honor locked scripts
and explicit exclusions; flag concerns without silently changing the contract.
