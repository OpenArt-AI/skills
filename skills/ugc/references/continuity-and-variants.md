# Continuity, changes and variant ownership

Before generating, distinguish three units:
- A shot is a visual or narrative beat in the design.
- A generation request is the unit the selected model produces and can regenerate.
- A deliverable is the complete video or explicitly requested set of clips.

Several designed shots can belong to one generation request. Several requests do not automatically make one
finished ad. Prefer a complete model-generated video when the live capability supports the requested scope.
If a fixed duration or fidelity requirement cannot fit, explain the mismatch and resolve the output contract.
Do not silently shorten locked content or present unassembled clips as a finished advertisement.
Separate clips are appropriate when the user requests them or accepts that alternative; this workflow does
not stitch them locally.

For continuity, record product/SKU, label orientation, object count, physical state, actor identity, wardrobe,
location, lighting, color treatment, camera relationships, graphics style and audio character as relevant.
An object picked up must not duplicate; a closed product must not become open without an intended action.
Inspect generated anchors before reusing them. A boundary image can guide visual continuation but cannot
guarantee voice, music or physical continuity.

For a revision, record what changes, what stays and which requests or references are affected.
Reuse accepted assets and outputs where they are independently reusable. Keep the prior version and receipts.
Do not claim a local edit is isolated merely because the revised prompt changes one sentence: regenerating
a complete ad may also change its body, camera, product details and sound. Check the entire new output.
If exact preservation is required, verify a supported editing route before submission; otherwise surface the
limitation and resolve the choice rather than presenting an approximate remake as exact.

For variants, name the question each version explores, its variable, and intended constants. Multiple unrelated
concepts and a controlled one-variable comparison are different briefs. Do not force a fixed variant count.
Reuse verified original references; compare actual outputs for accidental differences. A controlled prompt
does not guarantee a controlled visual experiment. Do not claim conversion or preference gains without evidence.

## Type-specific continuity

Track who holds the phone, which hands are available, where the product was set down and its current state.
A shot change must not duplicate an object or reset an opened package without an intended action.
Keep creator identity, wardrobe, voice direction and practical light consistent across related takes.
For alternate openings, distinguish independently reusable raw takes from a complete regenerated video;
keep the underlying product fact and approved body explicit, and report unintended performance changes.
