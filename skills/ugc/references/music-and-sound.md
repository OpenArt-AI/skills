# Music that supports the recommendation

## Decide what the scene should feel like

Normally include a light background music bed in a UGC ad, even when a short brief says nothing about music.
Choose it from the product's character, the audience, the speaker's emotional delivery and the activity on screen.
Music should make the sharing pleasant and engaging without turning the person into a commercial announcer.
It supports a useful recommendation; it cannot repair a slogan-only script or invented experience.

Make the choice contextually. A playful discovery or brisk hands-on routine may benefit from a light drum groove;
a comforting or reflective demonstration may need a softer, airy texture. A warm, upbeat bed can still be sparse.
These are relationships to consider, not genre assignments by product category. Do not give every beauty product
the same piano track or every energetic product an aggressive beat. Choose only enough musical detail to guide
the intended feeling: energy, pulse, texture and how much space it leaves for the person.

## Recognize when music would weaken the format

Honor explicit no-music and dialogue-only requests. Street interviews, podcast conversations, documentary-style
exchanges and demonstrations where listening to the product is the evidence often work better with voices,
room tone and real activity sounds. Judge the actual treatment: a podcast excerpt may stay unscored while its
separately commissioned promotional intro can have music. A phone aesthetic or continuous shot alone does not
justify silence. Record a brief reason for choosing no BGM so omission is an intentional creative decision.

## Direct its relationship to speech and action

Prefer instrumental or nonverbal music under speech unless the brief gives vocals a useful role. The voice stays
clearly in front at normal playback volume. Leave breathing room around useful product sounds, reactions and
important words; ease the music back where it competes, and let it support pauses without filling every gap.
A drum pulse can give momentum without demanding fast speech, cuts or a gesture on every beat. Preserve natural
room tone and handling sounds; ambience alone is not the selected background music bed.

Give the cue an appropriate entry, consistent character and intentional finish within the runtime. Avoid a
disconnected music restart at a visual cut, a sudden loud CTA sting or an accidental cutoff under the final word.
Do not impose universal BPM, instruments, loudness numbers or a fixed fade duration.

## Carry the decision through generation and review

Include a compact sound direction in the treatment and every independently submitted prompt: music present or
deliberately absent, its mood/pulse/texture, its relationship to voice and product sounds, and its ending behavior.
Keep musical instructions outside the spoken script. Discover actual native audio support and use supported
settings; do not invent a separate BGM parameter. Music is generated inside the returned video, not promised as
a later local soundtrack edit. If the selected route cannot supply the planned audio, surface that limitation.

Listen to the full result. Verify that planned music is actually audible, suits the scene, leaves words clear,
and enters and ends coherently; also check that intentional unscored passages remain unscored. An audio stream,
music mentioned in the prompt or a transcript does not establish this. Missing planned BGM is a review failure,
not an automatic silent-format exception. Correct within the authorized scope or report the unresolved issue;
mark these checks unverified when listening is unavailable.
