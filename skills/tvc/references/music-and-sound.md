# Music as part of the brand idea

## Give the film a musical identity

Normally include a deliberately shaped, brand-level score in a TVC. Treat music as part of the concept from the
start, not a generic bed added after visual planning. Derive its character from the brand's promise, audience,
product sensation and the intended emotional movement of the film. Brand-level means purposeful and coherent;
it does not automatically mean orchestral, expensive-sounding, dramatic or loud.

Select a useful musical vocabulary: energy, pulse, sonic texture, spaciousness and a recognizable motif or
gesture when it serves the idea. Playful percussion, a tactile rhythmic pattern, a warm melodic movement or a
sparse atmospheric texture can each fit different concepts. Do not map product categories to fixed genres, use
the same piano/strings for every premium brand, or direct only "cinematic background music."

## Shape progression and resolution

Plan how music introduces the world, develops with meaningful visual or product events and resolves with the
closing brand impression inside the runtime. A short film may need one concise musical phrase; a longer story
can sustain contrast and development. Choose a few meaningful synchronization points rather than accenting every
cut or forcing the pictures onto an arbitrary beat grid. Avoid a loop that ignores the film's progression.

Make space for dialogue, voiceover, tactile product sounds and environmental atmosphere. Under speech, keep words
clear and avoid competing sung lyrics unless explicitly intended. A visually driven section can let music lead;
a product sound or dramatic pause can briefly take priority. Do not force a constant bed through every second,
inflate every ending into a crescendo, or append a music tail beyond the delivery duration.

## Use silence as a creative choice

Honor a no-music brief. A deliberately unscored documentary or sensory concept can work when voices, product
sounds and silence carry a stronger brand idea. Explain that choice in the treatment; sparse input, fear of audio
generation or omission from the prompt is not a reason to silently drop the score. Local pauses or an exposed
sound moment do not require abandoning the musical identity of the whole commercial.

## Carry the score into the model output

Include music presence or deliberate absence, emotional character, pulse/texture, development and ending in the
sound direction of the approved treatment and generation prompt. Describe its hierarchy with voice, product
sounds and ambience. Use sufficient specificity to guide the film without hardcoded BPM, instruments, loudness
numbers or a universal cue structure. A sound reference supplies relevant traits, not a promise to preserve its
exact recording or synchronize generated footage deterministically.

Verify the live route supports the planned native audio and set only supported controls. Generate the music
inside the commercial; this skill does not add a separate soundtrack or local mixing pipeline. Resolve capability
gaps rather than returning a silent version as equivalent. Independently generated variants can share musical
direction without guaranteeing the same track or exact timing.

Review the whole film by listening: is the planned score present, specific to the brand impression, coherent
through transitions, appropriately balanced and resolved with the ending? Check competing vocals, distortion,
accidental restarts and cutoffs. Missing planned music is a review failure even if speech and visuals work.
Correct within authorized scope or report the gap; prompt text and an audio stream are not listening evidence.
