# Establish product truth before making claims

Inspect provided images, captures, product information and brand assets. Separate visible evidence,
documented facts, approved copy, assumptions and unknowns. A compact ledger can record each proposed claim,
its source, the product/SKU it applies to, and the picture or wording that will communicate it.

Lock the relevant product silhouette, proportions, structure, color, material, hardware, label placement,
packaging and quantity. Track each SKU separately. Do not combine the lid of one variant with the body of
another, invent a reverse side, or expose internal construction that no source establishes.
Choose a supported viewpoint when extra views are unnecessary; request missing views only when the intended
shot needs them. Generated reference images are creative assets, not new evidence of product facts.

Only infer properties that are actually visible. A visible seam supports "Seam Detail"; appearance alone
does not establish waterproofing, durability, capacity, health benefits or certification.
Do not invent prices, discounts, stock, reviews, sales figures, consumer experiences or endorsements.
Icons, badges, before/after scenes and demonstrations can also imply claims and require evidence.
An artistic metaphor must remain distinguishable from a test result.

Preserve approved wording and flag conflicts rather than silently rewriting a locked claim.
For new copy, use substantiated facts or remove unsupported assertions. Verify time-sensitive information
when it matters to the task through accessible authoritative material; record unavailable evidence honestly.
For software, use supplied or inspected real interface states. A generated imitation is not factual UI proof.

Use supplied logos and verified names when brand presentation is requested. A visible physical logo does
not automatically require a separate end-card wordmark. Keep packaging text, added brand graphics, slogans,
feature labels and dialogue captions distinct. "No added text" can coexist with authentic packaging text;
resolve a genuine conflict with the requested visual outcome instead of erasing product identity.

A logo reference is not a guarantee of exact generated lettering. Plan exact text explicitly where needed,
inspect the actual result, and report errors. Do not defer essential correctness to an unspecified post-production
stage. Consult [sequence and prompts](sequence-and-prompts.md) for placing text in the intended shot.
