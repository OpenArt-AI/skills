# Choose the right way to explain the product

Design for a shopper already inspecting the item alongside product photos. Start with clear product
recognition and reveal information that the still images do not explain well: an operation, scale in use,
construction detail or included parts. Keep the exact SKU consistent with the companion image gallery.
Avoid unnecessary brand-film buildup, unrelated creator storytelling or a feed-style attention stunt.
When a cover is requested, plan a legible product-focused frame and distinguish it from the video asset.
Do not claim the opening frame will automatically become the marketplace thumbnail.

Choose a presentation based on what a viewer needs to see, not a compulsory layout:
- Complete-product showcase for shape, appearance and recognition.
- Material/detail study for visible construction, finish, texture or craftsmanship.
- Usage demonstration for a supported operation and its outcome.
- Specification/accessory explanation for verified dimensions, variants or included items.
- Contextual use for scale, handling and a relevant real setting.
- Annotated feature explanation when concise words and pointers improve understanding.

Combine approaches when useful, keeping information legible. A product-focused video need not be white,
silent, graphic-led or free of people. A hand or person may explain handling and scale without turning the
video into a presenter-led advertisement.

## Select evidence and information order

Choose a few meaningful facts, reducing their number for short runtimes. Each demonstrated benefit needs
visible or documented support. Show the detail that makes the wording credible.
An attractive surface does not prove waterproofing, comfort, strength or longevity.

An overall view followed by details and a return to the product is often useful, but not mandatory.
A usage instruction may instead follow the actual order of operation. A single-feature video can stay on one
clear action. Do not repeatedly show a complete product without revealing the promised information.

Plan views supported by source material. Avoid unnecessary rear views, exploded constructions or disassembly
when evidence is missing. Real-world operations need coherent hinges, closures, pressure, contact, weight
and object states. A visual transformation must not imply a feature the product lacks.

## Photograph the product accurately

Keep color, silhouette, proportion, structure and visible labels faithful across views.
Choose light to explain form and material while controlling glare. Retain credible contact shadows, texture,
reflection and translucency. Avoid dramatic blur or repeated camera angles that prevent inspection.

Use purposeful motion: reveal an actual detail, establish scale, explain an operation or return to recognition.
Do not orbit by default, especially when source coverage is incomplete.
Separate physical product motion, camera motion and graphic movement in the direction.

## Use annotations only when they help

For a feature-callout treatment, connect each selected feature to visible evidence, exact short wording and
an intentional marker. A dot, fine leader and label is a useful default for that treatment, not for every
ecommerce video. A no-added-text showcase should remain clean.

Prefer specific, grounded wording such as a visible construction detail over "Premium Quality."
Use concise labels in the requested language; length is governed by readability rather than a fixed English
word count. Separate product labels from dialogue captions and decorative graphics.

A pointer must connect to the correct detail and stay connected during motion. Specify its anchor, path,
label position, entrance, stable hold and exit. Dimension lines require real endpoints, confirmed numbers and
units. Color chips, accessory quantities and icons must correspond to the actual offer.
Do not add badges, stars or certification-like symbols without evidence.

Use a consistent typographic/graphic treatment within a version. Reserve readable space without obscuring
product shape, branding or operation. Do not animate everything continuously; stabilize information while
the viewer reads. For complex motion, prefer a clearer supported camera/action plan over a fragile annotation.

## Sound and ending

Use product/operation sounds, ambience, speech, restrained music or silence according to the brief.
Normally include a fitting light music bed under [music and sound](music-and-sound.md); preserve meaningful
operation sounds and explicitly unscored treatments. Silent comprehension does not itself mean omitting BGM.
Clicks or ticks can support a callout or operation without overwhelming it. For a listing/detail video,
check that essential product information remains understandable without sound when that is the intended context.

End with a recognizable product, a completed operation, or an appropriate verified next step.
Do not invent offers, add an unrelated brand card, or leave a demonstration in an ambiguous halfway state.

## Turn inspection into a useful buying answer

Carry the viewer from identifying the exact item to seeing the detail or operation that answers a
buying question, then resolve the answer. Pair a named feature with the corresponding visible action
instead of repeating a full-product view. Suggested desk, wardrobe or gifting situations can clarify
relevance without being claimed as measured results. Borrow demonstration logic from references,
not an unrelated feature, variant lineup or unsupported endorsement. Keep the listing purpose primary.
