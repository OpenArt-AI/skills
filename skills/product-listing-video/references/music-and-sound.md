# Music that supports product inspection

Normally include light, product-appropriate background music in a listing video, including a visual-only
showcase with no narration. Choose energy, pulse and texture from the item's character, use context and pace
of the demonstration. A practical sequence may benefit from a modest drum pulse; a gentle tactile showcase
may suit a soft, airy or warmly upbeat bed. Do not assign every product a generic corporate loop or make
category-based instrument rules. Music should make inspection pleasant without competing with useful detail.

## Preserve the evidence and the intended viewing mode

Speech, mechanism sounds and acoustic comparisons take priority when they help a shopper understand the item.
For an audio product, a noise demonstration, a quiet operation test or a sound-focused sensory passage, lower
or omit music as needed; do not mask the very sound being evaluated. A generated soundtrack cannot establish
real measured loudness or performance. Preserve explicit no-music or source-audio constraints. Interview and
podcast-style portions may likewise benefit from natural sound alone. Record why an unscored choice serves the
commission instead of silently forgetting BGM.

A gallery may be viewed on mute. Keep essential product information understandable visually when that is the
intended context, but distinguish muted playback from an instruction to export a silent file. No presenter,
no captions or a clean background does not mean no music. Verify actual placement requirements through the
existing intake process rather than inventing a marketplace-wide rule about audio.

## Direct the sound with the demonstration

Prefer instrumental or nonverbal accompaniment beneath narration unless vocals have an explicit role. Keep
words clear, product sounds recognizable and the bed perceptible but unobtrusive at normal playback volume.
Leave space for reading and inspection; do not use rhythm to force faster handling, constant cuts or a sound
effect on every feature label. Natural ambience is separate from background music.

Describe an appropriate entry, sustained character and complete ending with the resolved product view or
operation. Avoid an inflated sales sting, abrupt loop restart or accidental cutoff. Use musical relationships
and qualitative energy rather than universal BPM, instruments, decibels or fade durations.

## Carry the choice into native generation and listening QA

Include music presence or deliberate absence, mood/pulse/texture, relationship to narration and product sounds,
and ending behavior in the treatment and complete generation prompt. Keep instructions separate from spoken
or visible copy. Verify the selected model supports the intended native audio and use only supported settings.
This workflow generates sound inside the video; it does not add a local soundtrack or mixing stage. If the
route cannot deliver the chosen sound, resolve or report that limitation rather than silently omitting it.

Listen to the complete returned video. Check that planned music exists, fits the product, preserves speech
and meaningful operation sounds, and ends coherently; confirm that deliberate unscored sections stay unscored.
Missing planned BGM is a review failure, not an automatic exception for listing videos. Review muted clarity
separately when required. Correct within scope or record the gap, and leave audio judgments unverified when
listening is unavailable. A present audio stream or successful generation is not sufficient evidence.
